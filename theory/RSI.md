## Relative Strength Index


It is a method used in technical analysis to measure the speed and magnitude of recent( or relative ) price changes.

RSI evaluates whether an asset is overbought or oversold on a scale from 0 to 100.

## Context 

* If RSI is greater than 70 than it will be considered Overbought
* If its less than 30 it is considered under bought
* If its around 50 then its considered to have neutral momentum.In an ongoing uptrend, RSI often stays above 50, while in a downtrend, it tends to hover below 50.


## Formulas:

$$RSI = 100 - \left( \frac{100}{1 + \text{RS}} \right)$$

where RS =  relative strength is calculated as follows:

$$\text{RS} = \frac{\text{Average Gain of Up Periods Over the Specified Period}}{\text{Average Loss of Down Periods Over the Specified Period}}$$


## Why?

* Simply to identify over/ under bought regions or regimes.
* Confirming a trend
* Divergence: Looking for a disconnect between price action and RSI movement (e.g., if price makes a new high, but RSI makes a lower high, it CAN( not always ) signal weakening momentum and an impending reversal)


## Noted Limitations:

* In strong up trending markets RSI could sit at above 70 for a long time, and vise versa
* Its calculated using a look back window, which of course is just data hence, it reacts to past price changes, it is inherently reactive rather than predictive.
* It can be all over the place in sideways price movements, i.e the RSI its self will posses a volatility of its own, so be careful.
* It is simply an indicator, it doesn't react to news or anything else, so dont base ur strategy just on this.


## How to minimize some these limitations:

* **Trend Filters:** Only taking RSI oversold signals if the broader market is in an overall uptrend (e.g., using a 200-period moving average as a filter).
* **Volume Analysis:** Confirming whether momentum shifts are backed by heavy volume or just low-liquidity noise.
* **Supports and Res:** Support and resistance levels highlight where massive pools of liquidity are sitting. When price pushes into these areas, the RSI stretches into extreme overbought or oversold territory, confirming the momentum is maxed out and the market is bound to reverse.
